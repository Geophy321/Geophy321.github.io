Title: Me and My Son Are on the Same Page
Date: 2026-10-24
Summary: Learning Swedish from ABC at the same time as my one-and-a-half-year-old son, and what a new language gives back.
Cover: images/swedish-book-cover.jpeg

<div markdown="1" style="text-align: justify;">

This autumn in Stockholm, my one-and-a-half-year-old son and I began learning Swedish together. He started förskola; I started from ABC. Without planning it, we found ourselves turning the pages of the same book.

I have learned languages before. Portuguese, French, a little Italian, each belonging to a different season of my life. Yet every language asks the same thing of you first: that you become small again, set down everything you know, and name the world one word at a time. With Swedish I feel that smallness alongside my son, who is meeting his first words just as I am meeting mine.

I am fortunate in my teachers. One is a retired schoolteacher, and our long conversations have become a classroom of their own. From there the words travel with me into the company, where I try them out, stumble, and try again. There is a particular satisfaction in carrying a language from the page into the mouth, from a quiet lesson into an ordinary working day.

Perhaps this is why learning a language feels less like study and more like rebirth. Each new tongue is a small reincarnation, another childhood offered to you, another chance to see the world as if for the first time and to find names for it. We are told we are born only once. Language gently disagrees.
